## The framing: human-scale AI
Three words carry it, and the setup is built around their being held together: **small, intentional, tailored.**
- **Small:** sized to a person, not a platform. Most of Nenya uses no AI; the one AI part is short and capped; every part can be read whole.
- **Intentional:** every use of AI is a decision a person can see and someone made on purpose, and what is left out is chosen as deliberately as what is built.
- **Tailored:** fitted to a person in a particular moment, by their own choices, with no profile behind it.

The first three texts each lead with one word and touch the other two lightly. The blend gives them equal weight and says how they hold each other up: small enough to be seen whole, intentional enough to be trusted, tailored enough to be of use.

## 1. Small (1751 characters)
Nenya is a small tool, on purpose. It is a free, open-source place to breathe, notice what you feel and what you need, and say it plainly, sized to a person rather than to a platform.

Most of it uses no AI at all. A slow breathing animation, six sensory lenses (sight, sound, touch, essence, movement and pattern) and the learning pages run in the person's own browser. What is entered stays on the device and is gone when the tab closes. There are no accounts to keep.

One part uses AI, and it is kept small in every way we can: an optional coach with five exchanges, messages of at most 500 characters, and replies of a few sentences. It runs on an open model, Gemma 4, offered by Venice AI in a mode Venice labels private. A daily limit and a rest between conversations keep the total modest. It is not therapy, and it says so.

Small also means checkable. The rules that matter most, such as stopping a message that suggests a crisis before any AI sees it, are plain rules that run on the page and on our server, and they are published and tested. The site is short enough for one person to read, and a public register lists everything kept, why, for how long and who can read it. Running the coach on a person's own device, so that nothing has to leave it, is the direction we are working toward. It is not built yet.

There are no accounts, no advertising and no tracking cookies, and no data is sold or shared. Nenya is independent, accepts no equity investors, and gives no funder a say in its direction. ## 2. Intentional (1929 characters)
Nenya is built so that every use of AI is a decision a person can see, and a decision someone made on purpose.

The reflection tools use no AI. A slow breathing animation and six sensory lenses (sight, sound, touch, essence, movement and pattern) run in the person's own browser, and what is entered stays on the device.

The coach is optional and begins with a plain notice before the first message: it is an AI, it is not therapy, the words go through our server to Venice AI, which writes the replies, we do not keep them, and for now it is open to connections in the United States. Each choice about it was made deliberately and is written down in public. It has five exchanges, because a short conversation can be held and then handed back to a person's own life. Its replies are brief. It does not promise to remember anyone or to be there later, and it points people back toward the people they know. Safety is not left to the model: a message that suggests a crisis is stopped before any AI sees it, and the page shows emergency numbers instead. A signed copy of a conversation can be checked on the person's own device, and it marks the lines the site itself wrote.

Intent shows in what is left out as well. There are no accounts, no advertising and no tracking cookies, and nothing is built to bring a person back. A report or message a person chooses to send comes with a code to delete it, with no email. An Emergency Exit on every page clears what the device saved and leaves the site. Every kept item is listed in a public register, and a change to the terms is dated and archived.

## 3. Tailored (1879 characters)
Nenya is made for a particular moment: a person who is wound up, worn out or too full to think, who wants a few minutes to settle and put words to what they feel and need. It is not therapy and not a crisis service, and it says so where people will see it.

The tailoring starts with pace. Breathing begins at six seconds in and six out, with other rhythms to choose and a pace of your own to set, and a person may stop there. Reflection offers six sensory lenses (sight, sound, touch, essence, movement and pattern), because people reach their inner life by different roads. A person picks up whichever fits, in any order, or none. Choosing fewer can be more helpful than choosing many, so the site says so, asks before a fourth choice on a card, and never blocks. Everything is optional, and extra detail waits under "More descriptions". The text is plain, and the site is designed toward the highest level of web accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AAA), with known gaps listed in public. It is built for a keyboard and a screen reader, respects a request for less motion, and offers color-blind modes and three text sizes.

The optional coach is tailored the same way. It listens for the feelings and needs the person names, uses the words of the Center for Nonviolent Communication's Feelings and Needs Inventory, credited, keeps its replies short and plain, and turns a person back toward the people in their life.

The site keeps no profile of a person across visits, so the tailoring is the person's own choices, made in the moment. There are no accounts and no tracking cookies. Nenya is independent, accepts no equity investors, and gives no funder a say in its direction. ## Before you use any of it
1. **Timing of the words.** The texts say "lens", "pattern" and "choosing fewer can be more helpful", which describe the tray and the lens words. They are on in dev and off in production until you flip `VITE_LENS_TRAY` and `VITE_LENS_COPY`. If the page text goes live before the flip, use "gateway" and "insight" and drop the sentence about choosing fewer and asking before a fourth choice.
2. **Venice wording.** The model is described in the Terms as Gemma 4 26B A4B "Uncensored", Venice's word for a model with few built-in refusals. These texts avoid the word and say the model is "offered by Venice AI in a mode Venice labels private". If you want the word in, say what it means (the limits come from Nenya's own checks, which are simple rules that can miss things), as the Terms do.
3. **Left out on purpose.** The optional "Request a summary", the running "What has come up so far" and the tapestry and path activities are not mentioned: they are off in production or not built. Moving the coach onto a person's own device is described only as a direction. The Terms-first entry and the plain-English summary are not mentioned either; add a sentence when they are live.
4. **Their approval.** I do not know Fractured Atlas's requirements. The texts describe an AI feature, outside services (Venice, Netlify, Cloudflare for the human check) and what the site keeps. Ask their project manager whether a change to the public description needs prior approval, and send the chosen text for their review first.
5. **Donations.** The texts say donations are tax-deductible as the law allows and do not list what they pay for. Add uses (hosting, AI processing, testing and review) only if you and they confirm them. Subscriptions are not mentioned in any text; the older Voice I did, and its sentence needs their view before it returns.
6. **No professional has reviewed the coach's safety checks.** Nothing here implies otherwise; keep it that way. The one claim to keep exact is "plain rules that run on the page and on our server": true of the crisis stops, while a small screening model, if used, is only a second check.
7. **Names and identifiers.** None of these texts says the scrubbing "removes" names: the page and the server replace common identifiers with placeholders and miss many names. If you add a sentence about it, keep "replaced with placeholders" and "names are often missed".
