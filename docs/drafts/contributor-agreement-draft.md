# DRAFT: Nenya contributor agreement

Status: a draft by the owner's request, written 2026-11-08 by Estëlle with Claude (Anthropic's AI) as co-author. **Not legal advice, and not in force.** Nothing here applies to anyone until the owner has finished it, a lawyer (and Fractured Atlas, if it wishes) has read it, and it is published. Until then Nenya has no contributor agreement, and the site's notices say so (`docs/translation-scope.md`).

**One clause is open.** The owner's note on collective and Indigenous knowledge stops mid-sentence ("…due unto the descendants, but also…"). Section 10 holds my draft of what was said and a marked gap for the rest.

## In plain words (read this first)
- You keep the copyright in what you contribute: a translation, a fix, a sentence, a page.
- You let everyone use, share and adapt it under the same open license the project already uses (the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later), so it stays open and cannot be locked away, including by us.
- You promise it is yours to give, and that you did not copy what you do not have the right to share.
- It has to fit the project's core stance: no tracking, no extraction, no advertising, privacy by design. We may decline anything that does not, and we owe no one a place in the project.
- Translations of safety-critical text (crisis resources, the coach's safety wording) are reviewed by a native speaker and tested before use.
- You are credited the way you choose, or not at all. Because the license is open, copies already shared cannot be taken back, but you can ask us to stop including or crediting it in future.
- We keep your contact details only to talk with you, and never share them.
- The name "Nenya" and the logo are not part of what you license: they stay reserved (`TRADEMARK.md`).
- All of this is acknowledged to rest on knowledge that belongs to many peoples and traditions (section 10).

## 1. Why this exists
Nenya aims for maximum accessibility, and translators, readers, and people with different knowledge of how people feel and speak are how it gets there. The code is public so anyone can check what it does; the words should be as shareable. This agreement lets people give their work to the project on terms they can read and trust, in keeping with its open, shareable posture. It does not make anyone an employee, partner, or owner of the project.

## 2. Words used
- **The project**: Nenya, operated by Estëlle as a sole proprietor and fiscally sponsored by Fractured Atlas, Inc., a 501(c)(3) public charity (*for the lawyer: which entity is the party to this agreement*).
- **A contribution**: anything you send in order for it to become part of the project: code, text, a translation, an image, documentation, a suggested change, or feedback.
- **The license**: the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later, the license of the repository's source (`LICENSE`).

## 3. You keep your copyright
You are not assigning your copyright to anyone. You remain free to use your own work however you like, including elsewhere and under other licenses.

## 4. What you license
You license each contribution to the project, and to everyone who receives the project, under **the license**, so that the contribution is shared on the same terms as the rest of the project. That includes the license's patent terms. This is a non-exclusive license: no one, including the project, gets exclusive rights in your work.

(*Choice for the owner and lawyer:* this is the simplest posture, "what comes in goes out on the same terms". If the project later wants pure prose, such as translations of the About pages, under a content license such as Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike, that is a separate decision and would be added here; it is not assumed.)

## 5. What the project does not get
Your license does not include the name "Nenya", the wordmark, or the gem and petal logo. They remain reserved as described in `TRADEMARK.md`, and nothing here lets anyone use them.

## 6. Your promises
- The contribution is your own work, or you have the right to contribute it under the license.
- It does not copy material you have no right to share. This matters in this project: for example, the feeling and need categories follow CNVC's Feelings and Needs Inventory, used with credit, and its wording is not ours to relicense. Do not paste copyrighted text, or text from a source whose terms forbid it, without saying so.
- It contains nothing harmful: no malicious code, no hidden tracking, no personal data you were not entitled to share.
- You will tell us if you find that a promise here was untrue.

## 7. The project's core stance
A contribution must respect what the project stands for, which its Structure page names: **anti-extractive design** (no data mining, no surveillance, no advertising), **privacy as architecture**, **free for everyone** at its core, **sovereign direction**, and **open source**. In practice that means a contribution may not add tracking, advertising, third-party scripts, or collection of what visitors write or choose beyond what the Terms describe, and a translation may not change what a sentence promises. We may decline, edit, or remove any contribution that does not fit, and we owe no one a place in the project. Welcome contributions respect that stance and are open and shareable.

## 8. Translations and safety-critical text
- **Faithful.** A translation says what the English says, no more and no less. If a sentence cannot be translated faithfully, say so and propose wording; do not soften or sharpen what it promises.
- **Reviewed.** Before a translation is shown, a second reader who knows the language reads it.
- **Careful parts.** The crisis resources are specific to each country and must be checked as real and current. The coach's safety wording and phrase lists are written and tested per language by a native speaker, and are not accepted as plain translations; the list of crisis phrases stays private.
- **Terms.** A translation of the Terms of Use & Privacy Policy is a courtesy copy, labelled as such; the English governs unless a legal check says otherwise.
- **The coach.** A translated page in front of the English-only coach says so, until the coach has passed its own evaluation in that language.

## 9. Credit, changes, and what cannot be undone
You are credited as you choose (name, handle, or not at all) in `ATTRIBUTIONS.md` and the credits page. You can ask us to change or remove your credit, and to stop including your contribution in future versions. Because the license is open and irrevocable for copies already shared, we cannot recall copies that already exist. We will not remove a safety fix from the project because of a withdrawn credit.

## 10. Collective and Indigenous knowledge (OPEN: the owner's clause, unfinished)
**What the owner asked for, as I understood it:** that this agreement state that the knowledge Nenya draws on is Indigenous in nature and in its rights of ownership, and that, as the product of collective effort, it is necessarily due to the descendants, "but also…" (the rest of the sentence did not arrive).

**My draft, for the owner to correct and complete.** I have written it as an acknowledgment and a set of commitments, not as a claim that gives rights to named people, because (a) a legal assertion that rights are held by "the descendants" would need a lawyer, including how it sits with the open license, and (b) I cannot verify that *all* of the knowledge in the project is Indigenous: parts rest on sources with known authors and owners (Tolkien's works, CNVC's inventory, academic research), whose rights belong to them and are credited separately.

> **Acknowledgment.** Much of what Nenya draws on, practices of attention, breath, reflection, naming what we feel and need, and ways of holding a community, did not begin with this project. Many of them are rooted in Indigenous and other living traditions, whose knowledge is made and held collectively, and carried by communities and by their descendants. This project does not claim to own that knowledge and does not treat it as a resource to be taken.
>
> **Commitments.** Nenya will credit its sources where it knows them and say so where it does not; will not enclose what is collective, which is why it is open, shareable and copyleft; will honour a request from a community or its representatives to correct, qualify, or remove how its knowledge is described or used; will hold what it makes in stewardship for the benefit of future generations, who are the descendants of the people who made the knowledge, and not for private gain; and will listen first to those whose knowledge it draws on, [***the owner's "but also…" belongs here***].
>
> **What this does not do.** This acknowledgment states how the project holds itself to account. It does not change the license that applies to the code and text, and it does not transfer or create property rights.

**For the owner:** finish the sentence; say whether "stewardship for descendants" is the right word or you mean something stronger (for example a duty to return something, or a share of any benefit); say which peoples and traditions you mean to name (naming some and not others carries its own weight); and whether to ask a person from those traditions to read this before it is used.

## 11. Nothing else
Contributing creates no employment, partnership, ownership, or payment. Contributions are provided "as is". To the fullest extent the law permits, neither side is liable for indirect or consequential harm. Where the law of a place gives a contributor rights that cannot be waived, those rights remain. This agreement is governed by the same law as the Terms of Use (the State of Minnesota, USA), *a choice the lawyer should confirm*.

## 12. Changes
We may update this agreement. A change applies only to contributions made after it is published, and the earlier text is kept in the repository.

## 13. How to agree
- **On GitHub:** add a line "Signed-off-by: Your Name <your email>" to your commit (the Developer Certificate of Origin style, widely used), which means you have read and agree to this agreement for that contribution.
- **By email, for people who do not use GitHub** (many translators do not): write to info@nenya.biz, saying "I have read the Nenya contributor agreement and agree to it for my contribution of [what]". We keep that email only to record it and to talk with you.
