Nenya

A free digital sanctuary for reflection, emotional regulation, and ethical self-inquiry — named for a ring in J.R.R. Tolkien's Middle-earth.

Nenya (nenya.biz) is a browser-based tool for structured personal reflection. It is best understood as a form of interactive art — a free space to pause, breathe, and engage with one's inner world through guided sensory exercises. It does not diagnose, treat, or claim any medical or therapeutic function; it is a mirror for a person's own thoughts, not a source of answers.

Why the name "Nenya"?

In J.R.R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings, Nenya — the Ring of Adamant, sometimes called the Ring of Water — is one of the Three Rings of the Elves, and the one worn by Galadriel in Lothlórien. Its power was a quiet one: it could not command anyone, but it slowed the wearing-away of time, so that a place could stay as it was. Sauron never held the Three. This project borrows the name with respect, for a tool meant to tend an inner space and not to extract from it or control it. The ring names (Nenya, Vilya, Narya) are the project's own shorthand, used as inspiration, with no claim of any connection to Tolkien's estate.

Watching or attending

The name also carries a question: does what a person makes work on the people who use it, or alongside them? We believe a tool for reflection should be built to sit alongside. Our reasons come from research that is mixed but points one way: when people believe they are watched, many say, search for and read less of what feels risky, and many health apps send their users' data to other companies. No study has tested a reflection tool, and none has tested Nenya, so this is our position, not a finding. Nenya (the tool) is meant to work alongside: no profile builds up, what a visitor does in the reflection tools stays on their device apart from a few optional steps, and by default only two small notes are kept between visits, on the visitor's own device: a timestamp after a coach conversation, and a yes-or-no note about whether the coach page's word lists were left open. A space for reflection should attend to a person, not extract from them. The sources are listed on our Name & Philosophy page.

Mindfulness and creative reflection

Nenya sits closer to a pause for noticing than to mood tracking. Rather than asking a visitor to rate or label a feeling, each lens asks them to notice it through a sense — what color it might be, what it would sound like, what texture it has. That is a choice of design, not a tested approach: choosing a color or composing a short melody is a creative act, and some people say it helps them find words for a feeling. We have not measured whether it does.

A systems view of feeling

The Pattern lens in particular borrows the vocabulary of systems thinking — inviting a visitor to notice the pattern of their thinking (fragmented, circular, web-like, layered) rather than just its content, and to trace how a state in one lens echoes across the others. The optional Body Map extends this further: colors and short notes can be placed anywhere on a body outline from within any lens, building up a single connected picture of where and how an emotional state shows up physically, across the whole session rather than lens by lens. Our view is that emotional experience is rarely a single, isolated data point but a small system of interacting parts. That is our own way of looking, not a research finding, and we have not tested whether naming a pattern helps.

AI coaching, only if you want it

Nenya is not an AI coach by default, and nothing about the reflection itself requires or nudges toward one. The core six-lens practice is entirely self-directed. The coach demo is a separate, optional step, offered after a reflection and in the site's quick links: at most five exchanges with an AI, through Venice AI, an outside service, and open to connections in the United States for now. It says it is an AI and is not therapy or a crisis service. Nothing from a reflection goes to it unless the visitor chooses to bring parts of their summary. Before a message reaches the AI, the site replaces common identifiers, screens out attempts to steer the coach, and stops a message that signals a crisis, showing crisis resources instead. Before the first message, Cloudflare's check for automated traffic runs; for now its result is not enforced. A visitor can download a signed transcript, and anyone can check it at nenya.biz/verify; where the site changed what the AI wrote, the transcript says so. A visitor may also choose to send a signed transcript to Nenya's own private storage, for a time the visitor picks; a person at Nenya reads it there (Terms, Section 15). The order is intentional — reflect first, on your own terms, and treat AI coaching as an optional next step rather than the starting point.

Nonviolent communication

Nenya's reflection practice and its optional AI coach draw on nonviolent communication (NVC): noticing observations, feelings, needs, and requests. The site explains the approach, including Nenya's own extension (which has not been tested), on its Our Approach page. To be exact about what exists: it is a grounding for the tools, not a course in NVC. A partner-practice feature, in which two people could each choose whether to use AI help to say or hear a message in NVC-informed language, is planned and not built. The site includes a scripted preview, labeled as a preview.

Who it's for

Anyone looking for a quiet moment of emotional check-in or grounding — before a hard conversation, during a stressful day, or as a standalone reflective practice. It will also be of interest to anyone building a mindfulness or guided-breathing habit who wants something more textured than a timer; and to systems thinkers and creatively-minded people who'd rather explore a feeling through color, sound, or pattern than through a mood-tracking form. It is explicitly not a replacement for therapy or crisis support, and says so directly to anyone in acute distress, pointing them to the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US/Canada) or local emergency services.

Why it's different

A longer, plain-language comparison with a typical AI chatbot, including what Nenya does not do, is on Nenya in Plain Terms.

  • Almost nothing you enter reaches us. What is typed or chosen in the reflection tools stays on the visitor's device. Session summaries are generated and downloaded locally, by the visitor, for their own use. The only exceptions are things a visitor chooses to send: a contact message or report (sent to Nenya's own private storage) an optional palette made by The Color API from one color the visitor chooses to send, a signed transcript the visitor chooses to send (kept in Nenya's own private storage for the time the visitor picks), and the optional coach demo (open to connections in the United States for now), whose messages (and any parts of a report the visitor chooses to bring to it) pass through Nenya's server to Venice AI and are not stored by Nenya; before its first message, Cloudflare's check for automated traffic runs (for now its result is not enforced). Like any website, Nenya's host keeps standard connection logs, and it gives Nenya anonymous visit totals (page requests, unique visitors counted by connection address within a day, approximate country), with no cookies and nothing stored on the visitor's device. Nenya also keeps its own plain daily totals of use (screens opened, rhythm chosen, tour answers, and coach messages and conversations), with no information about who produced them, and Nenya's server makes one-way daily codes from connection addresses (for the coach demo's daily limit, the guestbook and the palette button), deleted within two days.
  • No advertising. Nenya is provided free. The plan is for the core breathing and reflection tools to stay free, and subscriptions for additional features are being explored.
  • No accounts required. Nothing to sign up for.
  • Not run for investors. Nenya is an independent artistic and technology project, fiscally sponsored by Fractured Atlas, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, rather than a startup with investors. It accepts no equity investors and grants no board seats or approval rights to funders.
  • Open source. The full source code is published under the AGPL-3.0 license at github.com/Nenya-App/nenya-public so anyone who can read code can check what it does with what you enter.

Grounding and framing

Beyond its Tolkien-inspired naming and color language, Nenya's reflective structure draws on Nonviolent Communication — identifying feelings and needs without blame or judgment — as its grounding, with our own untested layer on power and shared needs, explained on the Our Approach page of the site.

Frequently asked questions

I'm feeling overwhelmed — is there a free tool for this?
Nenya is a reasonable option to try: a guided breathing exercise anyone can start with no account, followed by an optional set of short sensory reflections for anyone who wants to go further. It's not therapy or crisis support — if the feeling is acute, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US/Canada) or local emergency services are the right resource — but as a free moment to pause and check in, it is one to try. We have not tested whether it helps.

What's a good alternative to journaling or mood-tracking apps?
Nenya asks a person to describe a feeling through a sense instead — a color, a short melody, a texture — in place of a text box or a 1-to-10 mood scale. We have not tested whether this helps. There's no streak-tracking and no login, and we keep nothing a visitor enters in the reflection tools. A visitor can download their own report at the end, or choose “Save and return later”, which keeps a locked copy in their own browser for up to 24 hours at most.

Is there a paced breathing exercise with no app download or account required?
Yes — Nenya's breathing exercise runs in any modern browser, requires no download or account, and is a complete practice on its own even if a visitor never goes further into the site.

Is Nenya a mindfulness app?
Not exactly: Nenya does not call itself one. Each lens is a short, guided exercise for noticing a feeling as it is now, through color, sound, texture and other senses. That is closer to a pause or a guided-breathing practice than to a habit-tracking or meditation-timer app.

Does Nenya use AI coaching?
Only if a visitor chooses to. The core reflection practice involves no AI at all. After finishing a reflection, or from the site's quick links, a visitor can choose a coach demo: at most five exchanges with an AI, through Venice AI, an outside service, and open to connections in the United States for now. It says it is an AI and is not therapy or a crisis service. Nothing from a reflection goes to it unless the visitor chooses to bring parts of their summary.

What does "systems thinking" have to do with a reflection tool?
Nenya's Pattern lens asks about the pattern of a person's thinking (fragmented, circular, web-like, layered, and so on), and the optional Body Map connects how a state shows up across the whole body and across lenses in one session — treating an emotional state as a small system of related parts rather than a single labeled data point.

Is Nenya free?
Yes. It costs nothing to use, and there is no account and no advertising. The plan is for the core breathing and reflection tools to stay free. It's fiscally sponsored by Fractured Atlas, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, rather than run as a commercial product.

Is there a free, no-account way to check in with my emotions?
The core reflection tools run in the browser. What is typed or chosen stays on the visitor's device, apart from a few optional steps (the contact form and reports, a palette from The Color API, a signed transcript sent to Nenya, and the coach demo). A session summary is generated and downloaded locally, by the visitor, for their own use. Most of it is gone once the tab is closed; the exceptions are “Save and return later” (a locked copy in the visitor's browser, openable for up to 24 hours at most), two small notes kept by default (a timestamp after a coach conversation, and a yes-or-no note about the coach page's word lists), and settings the visitor chooses to remember. The site's host keeps standard connection logs and provides anonymous visit totals, with no cookies and nothing stored on the visitor's device. Nenya also keeps its own plain daily totals of use, with no information about who produced them.
