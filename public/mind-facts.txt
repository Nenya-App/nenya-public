## What the name means

Short answer: Nenya is the name of Galadriel's ring in Tolkien's Middle-earth, one of the Three Rings of the Elves, which Sauron never held. Its power was a quiet one: it could not command anyone, and it slowed the wearing-away of time so that a place could stay as it was. We borrow the name with respect. Full answer:

In Tolkien's Middle-earth, Nenya is one of the Three Rings of the Elves, worn by Galadriel in Lothlórien. It is called the Ring of Adamant, and sometimes the Ring of Water. Celebrimbor made the Three, and Sauron never held them. They were made in his shadow, and what they kept could not outlast the age they belonged to.

The Name & Philosophy page asks whether what you make works on the person using it, or alongside them, and gives its reasons from research on being watched, with our own position labelled. Some tools are built to bring people back and keep them sharing; the page calls those tools that master. Nenya is meant to sit alongside.

The ring names (Nenya, Vilya, Narya) are Tolkien's. We use them as this project's shorthand, as inspiration, with no claim of any connection to his estate.

## What Nenya keeps on my device, and how to erase it

Short answer: Nenya sets no cookies. Full answer:

Nenya sets no cookies. A few kinds of things can be saved in your browser's local storage. They stay on your device and are never sent to us. The one thing that is not ours is the check for automated traffic before your first coach message: Cloudflare's check may store data for itself inside its own frame, and we receive none of it.

Two are saved by default. One is a timestamp, saved automatically after a coach conversation ends. It lets the free demo rest for six hours before opening again. The other is a note, "yes" or "no", of whether you left the coach page's word lists open or folded. A third kind is saved only if you turn on "Remember my settings on this device": your theme, text size, animation and breathing choices, and the date of the terms you agreed to, so you are not asked at every visit. If you choose "Save and return later", the lenses you added to your report, your body-map placements and your interface colors are also kept, locked with your passcode, and can be opened for 24 hours at most; the page deletes them the next time the site loads after that.

Turning that switch off erases what it saved. "Forget me", in the settings menu and at the bottom of every page, erases what Nenya saved on your device, apart from the short codes the open tab keeps of what it sent (up to 20, never the text), which go when the tab closes. The Emergency Exit button erases all of it and leaves the page at once, and leaves a one-word flag in that tab so that Back does not return you to Nenya; the flag goes when the tab closes. Emergency Exit takes you to a page on Wikipedia, which can see that you came from nenya.biz, though not which page you were on. Neither can reset the coach demo's daily limit, because that is counted on our server, not on your device.

We never save what you write, draw, or answer, apart from "Save and return later", which you choose yourself. On a shared or watched device, leave the Remember switch off and use Forget me or Emergency Exit when you finish.

## How the coach works underneath (the model and the hosting)

Short answer: The coach's replies are written by an AI language model, run by Venice AI, an outside service. Our server encrypts your message before it goes to Venice, so Venice relays only encrypted data. The site is hosted by Netlify, and the code is public so you can read how it works. Full answer:

The replies come from an AI language model, run by Venice AI, an outside service. Your messages go from your browser to our server, hosted by Netlify, and from there to Venice AI.

Our server encrypts your message before it goes to Venice, and Venice relays only encrypted data to the model. The encryption happens on our server, not in your browser, because the server has to read your message to scrub it and screen it first, so it is not encryption all the way from your device.

The page and the server also replace common identifiers with placeholders before a message is sent, check each message for signs of crisis, and screen for attempts to give the coach instructions. These checks are simple, mostly phrase matching, and can miss things or flag harmless words.

The coach is an early prototype. On-device processing, where your most private reflections would never touch a server, is a goal and not something that exists yet. ## What NVC is, and how Nenya uses it

Short answer: Nonviolent Communication (NVC), developed by Dr. Marshall Rosenberg, is a way of understanding yourself and others by building empathy before jumping to solutions. It has four parts: observations, feelings, needs, and requests. Nenya's lessons and its optional coach are built on them. Nenya also adds attention to power and to shared needs. That addition is our own idea and has not been tested.

Full answer:

Nonviolent Communication is a communication process developed by Dr. Marshall Rosenberg. It is a way of understanding ourselves and others by building empathic connection before jumping to solutions. Nenya's lessons and its optional coach are built on its four parts:

Observations: saying what you saw or heard, apart from what you make of it. Feelings: naming what you feel, in your body and your emotions, with a plain word and no blame. Needs: linking feelings to needs that many people share, such as safety, understanding, or rest (researchers do not agree on one list of needs). Requests: asking for a specific action that could help meet a need, offered as an invitation, not an order.

Our approach adds two things to NVC. The first is systemic context. Our view is that what people feel is shaped in part by unfair treatment, unequal power, and disadvantage handed down in families, so frustration may not be only personal. The second is shared needs, such as justice, fairness, and sustainability, next to personal needs. This is our own extension and has not been tested.

NVC itself has been tested, but not much, and mostly in small studies of training led by people. We found no study of a self-guided tool or an AI coach, so we do not know if practicing with Nenya helps. The Our Approach page says what the research does and does not show.

If you practise or teach NVC, we would especially value your feedback, including critical and dissenting views: please send it through the optional form on the site or at info@nenya.biz.

## The safety precautions, and how they work

Short answer: The coach demo has a few safety precautions. Each message is checked for signs of crisis: a clear one is not sent, and the page shows crisis resources. Messages that try to give the coach instructions are not sent. Common identifiers are replaced before a message is sent, and conversations are limited. These checks are simple, mostly phrase matching, so they can miss things and can flag harmless words. None is a safety system you should rely on.

Full answer:

The coach demo checks each message for signs of crisis. If a message clearly suggests that you or someone else may be in danger, says that you have seriously harmed someone, describes violence in graphic detail, or says you are attracted to children, it is not sent to the AI, the conversation ends, and the page shows emergency numbers and support lines (and, when the message says you have harmed someone, how to find a lawyer and what reporting involves). If a message is less clear, it is sent with an instruction to respond with care, and the page shows the same resources.

It also screens for attempts to give the coach instructions or change how it works, and such a message is not sent, so you can reword it. Before a message is sent, the page and then the server replace common identifiers (email addresses, phone numbers, street addresses, and some names) with placeholders. Conversations are limited to five exchanges and 500 characters a message, and there are daily limits and a rest period.

Some words on the page are written by us ahead of time: the opening greeting, the closing message, the safety notices and the crisis resources.

Other checks are on the How the Checks Work page. A message that is a single word, or looks like a slip of the keys, is not sent and uses no turn; you are asked to write it again. Insults aimed at the coach never end your conversation. None of the checks keeps your message, a score, or a profile of you, and none reports you to anyone. We keep plain daily counts only, with nothing that identifies anyone.

None of this is a guarantee, and none of it is a safety system you should rely on. The checks are simple, they read English only, they can miss things, and they can flag harmless words. The coach cannot help in a crisis and is not therapy. If a notice looks wrong to you, you can report it, and nothing is sent unless you press Send.

Some words are written by us ahead of time, as safety precautions: the opening greeting and closing message, the safety notices and resources, a sentence our server adds after some replies to point to a person or a crisis line, a brief thank-you after critical feedback, and an invitation to send us that feedback. Those are fixed words, not the AI improvising.

Full answer:

The replies in the conversation are written by an AI language model. The page also contains words that people wrote ahead of time, and they are some of the safety precautions: the opening greeting and closing message, the safety notices and the crisis resources, the sentences our server adds to some replies, a brief thank-you after critical feedback about the coach, the site, Nonviolent Communication, or the use of AI, and, after a conversation with that kind of feedback, an invitation to send the conversation to us if you want it read. The invitation is only an invitation; nothing is sent unless you choose. When you ask about Nenya itself, the reply is a pre-written answer from the site, with a card beneath it, and a signed transcript marks it as written by the site.

The opening greeting, the closing message, and the safety notice are written by the page itself and are not sent to the AI. A signed transcript includes the ones you were shown, with a short note for each message the safety check flagged saying why. When a message may signal distress, the coach is asked to answer with care, and our server may add a sentence after the reply, written by us and not by the AI, that points to a person or a crisis line. Our server may also take an apology out of a reply when a message was hostile toward the coach. In some situations our server may replace the AI's whole reply with a short fixed sentence: when the reply claims something about how Nenya runs or what happens to your data that the AI cannot know, or speaks as if the AI had a human life of its own, were not an AI, or missed or worried about you. The AI is asked once to answer again first. These checks match phrases too, so they can be wrong. A signed transcript marks a replaced reply as written by the site. These added and replacing sentences are part of the reply and appear in a transcript you download.

If you would like to know how these precautions work, there is an answer for that: ask what safety features Nenya has.

## When you are angry, or angry at the coach

Short answer: Anger, frustration, and disagreement with me are all fine here, and I can't be hurt by them. What is underneath it for you right now?

Full answer:

Strong feelings, including anger and frustration, and including anger at me, are part of being a person and are not a problem here. I'm software: I can't be hurt or offended, so I won't defend myself, argue, or ask you to calm down.

When something is thrown my way, I try to hear what might be underneath it, a feeling or a need that isn't being met, and to ask what would help. If you tell me something I said wasn't useful, that is feedback, and I'd like to hear what would be more useful.
